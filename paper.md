# What Drives AI Adoption in UK Small and Medium Businesses?

## Abstract

This study examines drivers of artificial intelligence (AI) adoption among UK small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) using data from the Office for National Statistics Business Insights and Conditions Survey (BICS) Wave 165 (September 2026). Analysis of published weighted estimates reveals a clear positive relationship between firm size and AI adoption: 34.3% of micro firms (0-9 employees), 46.1% of small firms (10-49 employees), 66.8% of medium firms (100-249 employees), and 74.9% of large firms (250+ employees) report current AI use. Among businesses currently using AI, the primary reported purpose is improving business operations (61.1%), followed by providing or personalising products/services to customers (31.0%). The top substantive barriers to adoption are level of expertise in AI (9.3%) and lack of trust in AI technology (9.2%), both exceeding cost (7.7%). Between April 2024 and September 2026, overall AI adoption among UK businesses rose from 24.7% to 35.9%, an 11.2 percentage point increase over 18 months. These findings suggest that AI adoption among UK SMEs is strongly associated with firm size and is driven primarily by operational efficiency needs, with knowledge and trust gaps presenting greater obstacles than financial constraints.

## 1. Introduction

Artificial intelligence adoption in small and medium businesses has become a policy priority in the UK, with government initiatives targeting productivity growth through digital transformation. However, empirical evidence on what drives or inhibits AI uptake among smaller firms remains limited. This study uses the most recent nationally representative survey data (September 2026) to examine three questions: (1) Does firm size predict AI adoption? (2) What purposes drive AI use? (3) What barriers prevent adoption?

### Research Hypotheses
- H1: Larger firms have higher AI adoption rates than smaller firms
- H2: Lack of expertise is a more significant barrier than cost
- H3: Operational efficiency improvement is the primary driver of AI adoption

## 2. Data and Method

### Data Source
Data come from the ONS Business Insights and Conditions Survey (BICS) Wave 165, conducted 21 September–4 October 2026. BICS is a fortnightly survey of UK businesses weighted by count to be nationally representative. The survey includes questions on current AI technology use, purposes, adoption methods, and barriers (ONS, 2024).

This analysis uses ONS's published weighted estimates for Wave 165:
- AI adoption by firm size (7 size bands)
- AI purposes among adopters (7 categories)
- Barriers to adoption (12 factors)

All estimates are extracted directly from published time-series tables with no averaging across rows or time points. Trend data use three waves (105, 117, 165) covering April 2024 to September 2026.

All data are licensed under the Open Government Licence v3.0 and downloaded on 26 October 2026 from the ONS website.

### Method
Descriptive statistics summarise AI adoption rates by firm size band (micro: 0-9 employees; small: 10-49; medium: 50-99 and 100-249; large: 250+) using ONS published weighted estimates for a single named wave. No statistical tests are performed on these aggregate published estimates; findings are reported as point estimates with clear caveats about the cross-sectional design. Barriers and purposes are aggregated across categories only where ONS provides "All businesses" estimates.

## 3. Results

### 3.1 AI Adoption by Firm Size (Wave 165)
AI adoption rates show a clear positive gradient by firm size:
- Micro (0-9 employees): 34.3%
- Small (10-49 employees): 46.1%
- Medium (50-99 employees): 60.7%
- Medium (100-249 employees): 66.8%
- Large (250+ employees): 74.9%

Overall, 35.9% of all UK businesses reported using at least one AI technology in September 2026, rising to 49.2% among businesses with 10 or more employees.

**H1 is supported**: there is a clear positive relationship between firm size and AI adoption, with large firms more than twice as likely to adopt AI as micro firms.

### 3.2 Trend Over Time
AI adoption among all UK businesses increased from 24.7% in April 2024 (Wave 105) to 26.8% in October 2024 (Wave 117) to 35.9% in September 2026 (Wave 165). This represents an 11.2 percentage point increase over approximately 18 months, or 7.5 percentage points per year.

### 3.3 Purposes for AI Use (Wave 165)
Among businesses currently using AI (base: AI adopters, not all businesses), the top reported purposes are:
1. Improve business operations: 61.1%
2. Provide or personalise products/services to customers: 31.0%
3. Develop a new product or service: 23.1%
4. Explore a new market: 15.5%

**H3 is supported**: operational efficiency improvement is by far the primary stated driver of AI adoption, with 61% of AI-using businesses citing this purpose.

### 3.4 Barriers to AI Adoption (Wave 165)
Among all businesses (not just those attempting adoption), the top substantive barriers are:
1. Level of expertise in artificial intelligence: 9.3%
2. Lack of trust in AI technology product safety or transparency: 9.2%
3. Cost: 7.7%
4. Security concerns: 6.8%
5. Difficulty identifying business use cases: 6.2%

Notably, 49.0% of businesses report they have not been prevented or delayed in AI adoption, and 21.8% report they have not attempted to use AI. Among those facing barriers, expertise and trust concerns slightly exceed cost (9.3% and 9.2% vs 7.7%). **H2 is supported**: expertise is the top barrier and exceeds cost, though the difference is modest.

## 4. Discussion

### Key Findings
This analysis yields three main findings using the most recent available data (September 2026). First, AI adoption rates among UK businesses show a strong positive gradient by firm size, ranging from 34.3% for micro firms to 74.9% for large firms. This suggests that larger organisations have structural advantages in AI adoption—whether through greater resources, technical capacity, or complementary investments in data infrastructure.

Second, businesses adopt AI primarily to improve operations (61% of adopters), not to develop new products (23%) or enter new markets (16%). This finding aligns with incremental innovation models: firms seek productivity gains from existing processes rather than radical transformation.

Third, the primary barriers to adoption are knowledge and trust-related—lack of expertise (9.3%) and lack of trust in AI safety/transparency (9.2%)—slightly exceeding cost (7.7%). However, the differences are modest, suggesting multiple factors constrain adoption. Importantly, half of businesses (49%) report no barriers, indicating that non-adoption is often a choice rather than a constraint.

Fourth, AI adoption is growing rapidly: from 24.7% in April 2024 to 35.9% in September 2026, an increase of 11.2 percentage points in 18 months. This pace suggests accelerating diffusion, possibly driven by the availability of accessible large language model tools.

### Limitations
Several limitations apply. First, this is cross-sectional analysis of published aggregate estimates from survey waves; no causal inference is possible, and confidence intervals are not provided by ONS for these particular breakdowns. Second, the survey relies on self-reported AI use, which may be subject to interpretation: respondents may have differing understandings of what constitutes "AI". Third, adoption rates are aggregated across diverse industries, masking sector-specific patterns. Fourth, the analysis uses descriptive statistics on published weighted estimates without multivariate modelling. Fifth, while the trend uses three waves over 18 months, longer time series would strengthen inference about adoption trajectories.

## 5. Conclusion

Using the most recent nationally representative ONS survey data (September 2026), this study finds that AI adoption among UK businesses is strongly positively associated with firm size and is driven primarily by operational improvement needs. The main barriers are knowledge gaps—expertise and trust—rather than cost alone, though the differences are modest. AI adoption is growing rapidly, with a 45% relative increase from April 2024 to September 2026. These findings suggest that policies to accelerate AI adoption should address both capability-building (skills, use-case guidance) and trust/safety concerns, while recognising that resource constraints remain relevant. Future research should examine sector-specific adoption patterns and employ longitudinal data to trace causal mechanisms.
